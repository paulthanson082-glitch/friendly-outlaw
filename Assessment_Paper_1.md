# Assessment Paper #1: Biopsychosocial Assessment

## Client: "Jason Meyers" (pseudonym)

---

## Present Situation

### Living Situation

Jason Meyers is a 34-year-old white male currently living in a two-bedroom apartment in a mid-sized city. He lives with his girlfriend of three years, Sarah, and her 7-year-old son, Dylan, from a previous relationship. Jason works as an HVAC technician for a local heating and cooling company. He has been at this job for about two years which is actually one of his longer stretches of employment in one place.

### Presenting Problem

Jason was referred to treatment after receiving his second DUI in 18 months. As part of his court agreement, he was required to complete a substance abuse assessment and follow through with whatever level of treatment was recommended. During the initial intake interview, Jason admitted that he drinks "probably more than most people" but was hesitant to say he had a real problem. He stated he came in because "the court is making me" and that he "just had bad luck getting pulled over." However, as the conversation went on, Jason admitted that his drinking has been causing problems at work and at home. He said Sarah has threatened to leave him multiple times and that he missed about ten days of work in the last three months because of hangovers or still being too intoxicated to go in. He also admitted to using cocaine on weekends "sometimes" which he said has picked up in the last year or so. He described it as "just a party thing" but then said he spent around $400 on it last month which he could not really afford.

### Current Substance Use

Jason reports drinking beer and whiskey daily. He says on a typical weekday he will have "four or five beers" after work but admits that some nights it turns into more like eight or ten. On weekends he says he starts drinking around noon and continues throughout the day, estimating 12-18 beers plus mixed drinks when he goes out. He experiences blackouts about two to three times a month, mostly on weekends. He says he has tried to cut back several times on his own but always goes back to his old patterns within a week or two. He reports mild shaking in the morning that goes away once he has a drink, which suggests some level of physical dependence. He also reports sweating and difficulty sleeping when he tries to stop.

Regarding cocaine, Jason says he uses it on weekends, usually when he is out drinking at bars. He estimates using it two to three weekends per month. He typically buys a gram at a time and uses it throughout the night. He says it helps him "keep going" and drink more without feeling as drunk.

Jason smokes about a pack of cigarettes a day and has been smoking since age 16. He does not use marijuana currently, though he smoked it regularly in high school and his early twenties. He denies any IV drug use. He takes no prescription medications and has not been prescribed any controlled substances.

### Health Issues

Jason reports generally fair health but says he has had stomach problems for the past year or so, including acid reflux and occasional nausea in the mornings. He has not seen a doctor in over two years. He does not have health insurance through his employer and has not signed up for any marketplace plan. He reports frequent headaches, which he attributes to "not drinking enough water" but could also be related to his alcohol use. His appetite has decreased over the last several months and he estimates he has lost about 15 pounds in the last six months without trying. His sleep is poor. He says it takes him a long time to fall asleep unless he has been drinking, and when he does not drink he wakes up multiple times during the night feeling anxious and sweaty.

### Mental Health

Jason has never been formally diagnosed with a mental health condition. However, during the assessment he described symptoms consistent with anxiety and possible depression. He says he feels stressed out "all the time" and that he worries a lot about money, his relationship, and losing his job. He says his mood is usually "okay" when he is drinking but when he is sober he feels irritable and down. He admits to occasional thoughts that his life "isn't really going anywhere" but denies any suicidal ideation or self-harm. He says he has never seen a therapist or counselor before except for one session of couples counseling that Sarah made him go to about a year ago, which he did not return to.

### Education and Employment

Jason graduated high school with a regular diploma. He did not attend college. After high school he worked a series of jobs including fast food, warehouse work, and construction. He got his HVAC certification about four years ago through a trade program and has worked in the field since then, though he has had three different employers in that time. His current boss has warned him that if he misses any more work he will be let go. Jason makes about $42,000 a year before taxes.

### Financial Situation

Jason describes his finances as "a mess." He has about $3,000 in credit card debt and is behind on his car payment. He splits rent and utilities with Sarah, but she has been covering more than her share recently because of his spending on alcohol and cocaine. He says money arguments are one of the biggest sources of conflict in their relationship.

### Legal Issues

Jason's current legal situation includes the two DUIs mentioned above. His first DUI was about 18 months ago, for which he received a fine, a brief license suspension, and was ordered to attend a DUI education class, which he completed. His second DUI occurred two months ago, and this is the one that resulted in the court-ordered assessment. His license is currently suspended. He also has an old misdemeanor charge from when he was 22 for disorderly conduct which was connected to a bar fight while intoxicated.

### Religious/Spiritual Affiliation

Jason says he was raised going to church occasionally as a kid but does not practice any religion currently. He says he is "not really into that" but would be open to spiritual aspects of recovery if it did not feel too "pushy."

### Custody Issues

Jason has no biological children. Sarah has full custody of Dylan. Jason says he cares about Dylan and tries to be a good role model but admits that his drinking has made that difficult.

## History

### Substance Use History

Jason reports that he first tried alcohol at age 13, drinking beers stolen from his father's refrigerator with neighborhood kids. By age 15 he was drinking regularly on weekends at parties. He started smoking marijuana around the same time. His drinking increased significantly after high school when he turned 21 and could buy alcohol legally. He describes his early twenties as a period of heavy partying, drinking most days of the week and smoking marijuana daily.

He first tried cocaine at age 24 at a party and used it sporadically for a few years before it became more regular in the last year. He experimented with prescription painkillers (Vicodin, Percocet) in his mid-twenties when a friend offered them to him, but says he never bought them on his own and it was not a regular thing.

Jason says he first realized his drinking might be a problem around age 28 when his previous girlfriend left him partly because of it. He tried to quit on his own at that point and managed about three weeks before going back to drinking. He has never attended AA or any formal treatment program before. He says he always figured he could handle it on his own.

### Mental Health History

Jason has no prior mental health treatment other than the one couples counseling session mentioned above. He reports that he has felt anxious for as long as he can remember but that it got worse in his late twenties. He says he sometimes feels depressed, especially when he thinks about where he thought he would be in life versus where he actually is. He denies any history of trauma but later in the assessment mentioned witnessing his father being physically aggressive with his mother on several occasions as a child, which he described as "just how it was."

### Family of Origin (FOO) History

Jason grew up in a small town about two hours from where he lives now. His parents are Rick and Denise Meyers. Rick worked as a mechanic and Denise was a stay-at-home mom who occasionally worked part-time at a grocery store. Jason is the middle of three children. He has an older brother, Kevin (37), and a younger sister, Amanda (31).

Jason describes his childhood as "not great but not the worst." His father was a heavy drinker who would come home from work and drink beer every night until he fell asleep in front of the TV. Jason says his father was not around emotionally and that when he did interact with the kids it was often to yell at them or discipline them harshly. He recalls his father hitting him and his brother with a belt on multiple occasions, though he downplays this by saying "that was just normal back then." As mentioned, he also witnessed his father being physically aggressive with his mother, including pushing and grabbing her during arguments, usually when his father had been drinking heavily.

His mother, Denise, struggled with depression for much of Jason's childhood. He remembers her spending a lot of time in her room and being emotionally unavailable. She did not drink or use drugs as far as Jason knows. His parents divorced when Jason was 16 and his father moved out of state. Jason has had minimal contact with his father since then, maybe a phone call once or twice a year.

Jason's older brother Kevin also has a history of heavy drinking and was arrested for a DUI when he was in his twenties. Kevin currently drinks regularly but Jason is not sure how much. His sister Amanda does not drink much and is described by Jason as "the one who got out" because she went to college and lives in another state.

Jason's paternal grandfather was also known to be a heavy drinker, and Jason believes his father's brother (his uncle) had a drug problem at some point, though the family did not talk about it openly. There is a clear pattern of substance use and addiction in the family going back at least two generations.

### Educational and Occupational History

Jason was an average student in school. He got mostly Cs and a few Bs. He was not involved in extracurricular activities except for playing on the junior varsity football team for one year before quitting. He did not get into any serious trouble at school but was suspended once in 10th grade for showing up to school smelling like marijuana. He graduated on time and went straight into the workforce.

His work history has been inconsistent. He has held many different jobs and rarely stayed at one place for more than a year or two until getting his HVAC certification. He says his job performance tends to start strong but declines as his drinking picks up, leading to missed days and eventually either quitting or being fired.

### Legal History

As noted above, Jason has two DUIs and one misdemeanor disorderly conduct charge. He has no felony convictions. He was not in trouble with the law as a juvenile.

---

## Summary and Diagnostic Impression

Based on this assessment, Jason meets the criteria for Alcohol Use Disorder, Severe. He demonstrates tolerance (needing more to feel the same effect), withdrawal symptoms (shaking, sweating, insomnia when not drinking), repeated failed attempts to cut down, continued use despite it causing problems in his relationships and work, spending a significant amount of time obtaining and using alcohol, giving up activities he used to enjoy, and continued use despite knowing it is causing physical problems. He meets more than six of the DSM-5 criteria which places him in the severe range.

He also appears to meet criteria for Cocaine Use Disorder, Mild to Moderate, given his increasing frequency of use, spending more money than he can afford on it, and using it in combination with alcohol in a pattern that is escalating.

There are also symptoms that suggest possible co-occurring Generalized Anxiety Disorder and Depressive Disorder, though a more thorough mental health evaluation would be needed once he has a period of sobriety, since many of these symptoms could be substance-induced.

Jason presents with several risk factors including a strong family history of substance use disorders, early onset of alcohol use, exposure to domestic violence and harsh physical discipline as a child, limited education, and lack of prior treatment. Protective factors include his current employment, his relationship with Sarah and Dylan, and the fact that he is here completing this assessment even if it is court-ordered.

Further assessment and treatment planning will be needed to address Jason's substance use, mental health symptoms, family dynamics, and overall functioning.
